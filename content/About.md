### Origin

###### My Pensieve as a Digital Garden

The design here follows what I consider best for reading and thinking. I keep it minimal, aiming for a clean, book-like feel while using only essential tools to have a consistent experience across all devices.

I connect ideas through internal links whenever related concepts appear across different posts. These tinted hyperlinks include both internal references and category tags. 

The logic behind the design originates from my own learning process: I often find that a single core principle can apply to many different situations. Having a consistent way of thinking and acting largely shapes who I want to become. 

This doesn't mean clinging to rigid rules or suppressing creativity; rather, it’s a way to remain consistent and exploratory at the same time.

This hypertext keeps growing as long as I keep gardening. It helps organize my digital brain while sparking new ideas that might never emerge in a chronological format.

To me, one of the greatest losses is watching remarkable ideas fade away as time passes. It feels unfair to those thoughts. Every piece of experience and insight holds value, no matter how long ago it was formed. (That's how I feel about history, too.)

Gardening also allows room for seasonal change. When certain connections no longer resonate, I am free to trim, prune, or nurture new growth.

Ultimately, this Pensieve serves as my digital garden, a personal knowledge management space composed of opinionated, continuously evolving contents.

If you are curious to learn more about the concept of a Digital Garden, I recommend the readings below:
- [Networked Thought](https://jzhao.xyz/posts/networked-thought)— Jacky Zhao
- [A Brief History & Ethos of the Digital Garden](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history)— Maggie Appleton
