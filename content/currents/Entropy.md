### Thermodynamics
Entropy measures the degree of disorder (or unavailable energy) in an isolated system.[^1]

According to the second law of thermodynamics, natural processes are irreversible, for example, heat flows spontaneously only from a hotter region to a colder one. To reverse this process, external work must be done on the system.

When the system reaches thermodynamic equilibrium, where heat is distributed uniformly and disorder is maximized, it is in a state of high entropy. High entropy indicates that during heat transfer, a portion of energy becomes degraded into unrecoverable waste heat that can no longer be converted into useful work.

### Information Theory
Entropy measures the uncertainty or surprise of the information.

High entropy indicates higher randomness and variety, meaning each observation reveals a greater amount of information.
