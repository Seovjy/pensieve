It is never easier to produce than to consume, and this concept works in every field. It is like writing compared to reading, and learning compared to teaching. Throughout my studies, the concept became more reinforcing, while I was fully committed to learning new things. 

People tend to have short memories. Even though I paid my full attention in each class, that information and knowledge can't be stored in my mind eternally. They are scattered around with different formats no matter how well organized the files are.

Most of the time, I still rely on my limited memory. The faded concepts someday may come in for good use, then I may eventually find out that I stutter when I say,

"Oh wait, I know this...
It could be this... 
Let me clarify that...". 

It is a frustrating moment.

Therefore, I am searching for a **structure** and a **process** to locate more useful information and transform it into potential actions in my context of use.

## Writing as learning
First, I want to have an interconnected database completely established by myself. 

I learned the concept of LLM Wiki, but I am not a big fan of it. I experienced downsides like hallucinations, information compression or loss, or trustworthiness issue. Of course, agent models can be upgraded, provided with clearer instructions, and use [[Human and AI Collaboration#Human-In-The-Loop|Human-in-the-loop]] approach to resolve the aforementioned issues. But I have more faith in a DIY way in this regard.

It is critical for me to be the one who filters the necessary contexts in my vault, synthesizes various ideas into a cohesive perspective, and concludes with findings matching my own experiences. What matters to me most is the purpose of achieving deep learning and stronger memory traces. For that, some research based on neuroscience can explain it.

In neuroscience, here is a simple [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YzCYuKX6zp8&t=140s) that I found pretty helpful for understanding the dopamine reward pathway that supports how our brain thinks, reacts and stores the memories effectively. But dopamine doesn't just spike whenever we feel happy about something; it ramps up through your effort and friction accumulation, and through this process, we are able to keep focusing on our next motivation. [^1]

So when do I feel most satisfied and happy with learning? For me, it comes down to the accomplishment of being able to use that knowledge to fix some problems—I always desire that. Or what's more exciting is propelling new opportunities that are yet to come, which is rare and precious, and I am not sure if my brain can handle the afterwards dopamine celebration if it happens.

To deliver, we need a medium; that is either speaking or writing.  For me, they are different paces of building from scratch. But they all come from your thinking first. 

What's the purpose? 

What's my belief?

Who's the audience? 

How should I structure this?

Would it work out? Is there a better way?

All of these questions and ideas would pop up in my mind. That's the thinking process I am putting effort into. Unlike reading or listening, which passively accepting ready-made structures and interpretations, if I truly want to reinforce my learning, writing is definitely a scientifically proven and practical practice to closely examine my thinking. [^2]

I want to retrieve the knowledge that had resonated with me during this learning journey, to recapture those 'Aha' moments. Either from fundamentals or advanced analytics, they all connect in my mind, but yet I haven't put them in a good record. 

So I believe writing is a good way to justify my learning goals and results continuously.

> "Your success in life will be determined largely by your ability to speak, your ability to write, and the quality of your ideas. In that order." — Patrick Henry Winston

### Writing as sharing
Secondly, I will take writing as an output of my personal knowledge management[^3]. 

The purpose is to regularly share and inspire if possible. 

To disclaim first, the 'inspire' I am referring here is more like reciprocal. I believe most of the time people share out of similar reasons. Either they expect compliments (who doesn't like them?), or they look for your opinions to improve. 

That's the simple origin of publishing my work on Quartz, matching the [philosophy](https://quartz.jzhao.xyz/philosophy) when it was built.

It could be like a discovery or a half-made memo, but eventually I expect them to be connected in an expressive and knowledgeable way to also make the people who read it feel as inspired and accomplished as I do.

> “One who works with the door open gets all kinds of interruptions, but they also occasionally gets clues as to what the world is and what might be important.” — Richard Hamming
